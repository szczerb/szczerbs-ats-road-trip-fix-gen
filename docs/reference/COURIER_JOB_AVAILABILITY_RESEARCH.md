# ATS 1.61 Road Trip courier-job availability: online evidence

Inspected: 2026-10-18. Target: ATS 1.61 Road Trip (Ford release 2026-09-29), not the older truck or ETS2 markets. This is online research combined with previously recorded local definition findings; no game session, profile, archive, mod, or code was changed. **Documented** identifies a sourced statement: SCS engine/gameplay descriptions are distinguished from an add-on creator's own observations and uncontrolled player anecdotes; **observed locally** refers to the earlier static archive research linked below. None of those categories reveals the proprietary offer-generation algorithm.

## Finding relevant to the city's apparent job count

**Documented — user report, 2026-10-18:** more discovered cities seem to coincide with fewer cities offering many courier jobs and more offering none. This remains an uncontrolled observation; no fixed-city before/after counts demonstrate redistribution yet.

**Unknown:** No inspected SCS Road Trip post, SCS modding page, or publicly indexed source file specifies a global courier-offer budget, a per-city quota, how discovery changes offer generation, or a precise refresh and expiry schedule. The impression that discovering more cities dilutes a fixed global pool is plausible but **unproven**. A city added to the *visible list* could instead reveal a pre-existing low or zero count and lower the fraction of cities that appear productive without taking any offers away from a previously visible city. The sources also do not establish that city discovery itself starts a market refresh. Installed map cities and cities discovered in the profile are different quantities and must be measured separately.

SCS says [map exploration is shared across modes](https://blog.scssoft.com/2026/09/coaches-road-trip-mode-switch.html?m=1) (2026-09-25), but does not say how the Road Trip Quick Jobs or Dispatch Market use discovered-city state. SCS's [Road Trip courier preview](https://blog.scssoft.com/2026/09/road-trip-quick-jobs-freight-market.html?m=1) (2026-09-13) explains rating, vehicles, and the two markets without a quantity or discovery rule. The [release post](https://blog.scssoft.com/2026/09/road-trip-ford-dlc-release.html?m=1) (2026-09-29) confirms the released names Quick Jobs and Dispatch Market.

## Ranked explanations to test, not generator rules

1. **Eligibility and destination density (strongest static basis).** The source city may have too few effective origin contexts or destination contexts satisfying a candidate job's defined `company_distance` interval at the fixed rating and car class. A city can therefore have no live offer even when its company markers exist. The official rating/vehicle statements and the installed job definitions support these as inputs, but the distance calculation and engine selection remain unknown.
2. **Visibility and denominator effect (plausible, no direct algorithm evidence).** Newly discovered cities may expose previously unseen low or zero counts while the offer count at a fixed old city stays similar. Measure both absolute offers at fixed cities and the percentage of discovered cities with any offer; the latter alone cannot establish dilution.
3. **Refresh variation or expiry (partial author evidence).** Different observations may sample different market states. The Workshop authors report variable job types across refreshes, but no inspected source establishes the vanilla refresh cadence or offer lifetime. A before/after comparison should use the same observation window and multiple natural updates, without forcing a profile reset.
4. **Fixed global-budget dilution (weak, unproven).** If the game allocates a stable number of offers across all eligible discovered cities, adding cities could reduce per-city counts. No inspected primary source states that rule. It would require the fixed-city count to fall as the visible city set grows while vehicle, rating, market, filters, effective map/mod stack, game time, and refresh phase stay comparable. Seek counterexamples where newly discovered cities add visible offers while fixed-city counts remain stable.

Other map-specific causes, such as broken semantic company links, missing context, inaccessible routing, and private-alias context changes, remain candidates for individual depots. They cannot be inferred from a city's aggregate count alone. [Company editor filenames may carry DLC suffixes](COURIER_MARKER_RESEARCH.md); inspect semantic company/city/prefab data before declaring a missing link.

## Low-risk controlled observations for a later game session

No further manual tests are expected during this night's work. When testing resumes, use a copied profile or a backed-up test profile and record ATS build, active DLC and mod order, owned car/class, Courier Rating, game time, discovered cities, and visible market/filter/sort state. Do not alter the live profile or force an economy reset for this study.

1. Choose two already-discovered cities, one with several offers and one with few or none. Record **per-city departures** and **total visible market offers under the recorded filters** in Quick Jobs and Dispatch separately, with the same car and rating. Include origin/destination pairs, route length, and job type where shown; an owned-car Dispatch count cannot be compared directly to supplied-car Quick Jobs.
2. At the same fixed cities, record counts again after a natural market update at similar game-time offsets. Then discover one additional city without changing the mod stack and repeat the fixed-city and total counts over several comparable updates. Track the newly visible city's own count. For a stronger discovery comparison, start two copied-profile runs at the same checkpoint and allow equal game time to pass, discovering an extra city in one run and keeping discovery unchanged in the other. Keep rating and car unchanged; repeat because market randomization may still differ. A simple travel-before/after comparison confounds discovery with elapsed time. A stable visible total alone does not prove a fixed internal budget or reveal hidden offers. If only the percentage of productive visible cities falls, that supports a visibility effect; if absolute fixed-city counts repeatedly fall while a stable global total is reallocated, that supports the dilution hypothesis.
3. Compare a working stock city and a sparse map-mod city with similar candidate destination density at the same rating/car/market. Use the read-only `analyze-map --processor courier` report to identify context and company-link candidates, then compare candidate destinations against each job's defined `company_distance` interval, explicitly recording the distance method used for the estimate. This narrows a static mismatch; it does not prove the runtime distance or offer rule.
4. Repeat with the same approved mod stack and filters, changing only one factor at a time: rating tier, car class, or market. Do not interpret a single missing offer as an absent city rule. Record offers, pickup, reachable route and completed delivery as separate outcomes.

No verified online source for actual publicly posted Road Trip courier definition code or the ATS 1.61 offer allocator was found in this search. The two Steam Workshop pages are developer-authored descriptions, while the project has separately inspected installed Workshop definitions locally; neither is public engine source. The exact per-city/global generation budgets, discovery gate, refresh/expiry parameters, route distance calculation, and Road Trip-specific UI filter semantics remain **Unknown**.
