## Selection and limits

The new method extracts raised, near-flat boundaries near building proxies: weld 1 mm, remove interior edges, merge straight subdivisions, and corroborate three outside-ground samples with a 0.05–0.25 m rise. Signed building winding handles concave proxies; bays follow the observed boundary. A 0.5 m offset is an explicit geometric policy, not measured pedestrian or wheel clearance. Both perpendicular and parallel layouts are considered; parallel entry uses a longitudinal end.

Checks cover both footprint and approach: surface samples, visible model obstacles, door hypotheses, inaccessible UI proxies, native rest, parked-vehicle locators, map roads and pump-operation exclusions. Default policy uses a 6 m pump radius, 3.5 m road radius, 0.5 m obstacle margin, 4 m approach depth and conservative parked-car/truck radii of 3.5/10 m. These are inspectable policies in metres, not engine or highway-design standards. The model-specific functional storefront door, external signboards/furniture, collision meshes, pedestrian routes and full vehicle turning paths remain incompletely resolved. Named materials only bound feature searches; names or empty asphalt alone do not establish safe parking.

Passing candidates rank by separator evidence, raised-boundary evidence, minimum surplus over checked clearances (capped at 2 m and rounded to centimetres), pump clearance (capped at 20 m), bay count and stable ID. All rejection reasons and ranking components are reported. For painted candidates with two possible ends, the current access stage chooses a passing end by pump clearance before candidate ranking; it does not optimize a full turning path or compare end-specific minimum margins. This limitation does not affect the single-approach selected Dawson proposal or the already-covered native control.

C r1 is retained as an intermediate experiment and excluded from the recommended test bundle. Its checks passed, but independent recomputation found only 0.0025 m surplus over one obstacle-proxy exclusion; greater pump distance alone was a poor ranking objective. C r2 adds the clearance-surplus priority without relaxing exclusions. No full-frontage expansion is forced.

The three retained B images and complementary ground view support inspecting natural parking near the curb and possible access conflicts. They are not calibrated world-coordinate overlays. The plans' grey/green UI regions and dashed geometry are proxies; their colours do not certify physics or functional use. The 512 displayed obstacle-triangle cap omits additional drawn detail; candidate checks still process the relevant full mesh within the work budget.

## Activation: known and unknown

**Documented:** polygon Range is vertical; Sphere Trigger uses it as radius. Partial Activation allows boundary contact. The PPD bit is `0x4`; standalone trigger flags use a different schema. Primary links and the inspected ATS version are in the [research record](STATION_PARKING_HEURISTICS_1_61.md#activation-evidence).

**Unknown:** which vehicle point or shape ATS tests, its containment/contact tolerance, the exact vertical semantics and heading effects. The three B images/log show prompt transitions without calibrated vehicle coordinates. They do not identify a centre, bumper, wheels or full-body test. The native inventory (31 car groups / 124 vertices) and A/B all use flags 0, range 3 and reset 0/0. No explicit yaw field is written. C retains these defaults; its arrow suggests physical parking direction rather than an engine heading requirement. No predicted valid-car-pose envelope is claimed.

E is a minimal later comparison: at rest, cross one long edge and one short edge in small steps using the same vehicle reference; repeat with 0°, 180° and 90° headings. Compare B versus E with vehicle, stack, speed and engine/fatigue conditions held constant. A wider contact window would support a contact-versus-containment effect; heading dependence may indicate an extended shape. Record unexpected behaviour too. Do not combine E with C when interpreting this comparison.
